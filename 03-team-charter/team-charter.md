## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** We own the experience of existing Fable users aged 25-30 in NY, LA and Chicago: helping them sleep well through difficult periods and find self-care beyond sleep. Growth owns getting new users in the door; we own what happens once they are here.
- **What is out of scope.:** What Growth owns (not us)
• Acquisition channels, paid and organic, and anything before an account exists.
• New-user growth targets and experiments aimed at new users.
• When #1 Onboarding redesign moves from Later to Next, ownership is decided jointly with Growth at that point (see joint calls).
Explicitly out of scope
• #2 Daily streaks and #3 Social layer. Off the roadmap; they conflict with the winning aspiration and the privacy-first hard no. New proposals that resemble them start at the product lead.
• Clinical care. #9 Crisis mode and #5 Therapist-matching do not get built until clinical scoping is complete. No PM ships anything that reads as clinical advice.
• Markets and segments outside where-to-play: #7 Spanish/Portuguese, #8 Web version, #13 Fable for Teams stay in Later until their revisit triggers are met.
• Production incident response. Engineering on-call owns diagnosis and resolution. PMs own customer communication and impact assessment, and escalate through engineering leadership, not by fixing.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** 1. Where or when the check-in opt-in appears in onboarding
2. Check-ins delivered through notifications, or any change to the notification budget (how many, how often, what time)
3.A new use of historical user data, or a change to consent language
4. Adding, removing or reordering onboarding steps that affect activation or new-user conversion
5. An experiment whose expected effect lands on another owner's primary metric
6. Moving #1 Onboarding redesign forward, or any Growth experiment inside the logged-in app
7. Pulling capacity from #10 accessibility
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** The owner of an area decides inside it. Overlaps go to a named joint call, then up a fixed escalation path with a clock on every step, so a team-only conflict is settled within 5 business days and a cross-team one within 15.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** 1. Customer outcome. Which option better helps a 25-30 professional sleep well through a difficult period?
2. Strategic metric. Which option moves the metric tied to a Now item (sleep-quality check-ins, churn and MAU, non-sleep adoption)?
3. Differentiator. Which option strengthens personalization from our historical data without weakening privacy-first?
4. Reversibility. If still tied, choose the option easier to undo and set a date to review the data.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** 1. Owners resolve directly, within 2 business days. The PM who raised it writes a one-page decision note: the question, the options, the effect on each owner's metric, and a recommendation. Owners meet once and apply the tie-breakers.
2. Product lead decides, within 3 business days. If owners cannot agree, either one adds it to the weekly product sync or sends it async. The product lead decides with both owners present and records the reasoning.
3. Product lead and Growth lead decide jointly, within 5 business days. Used only when Growth is a party. Both leads meet with the decision note and the relevant owners.
4. Executive sponsor decides, within 5 business days. If the two leads cannot agree, they bring one shared decision note showing both positions to the executive sponsor. That decision is final for the quarter.
